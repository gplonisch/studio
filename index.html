The checks are not a promise, they are a build step

Every site I ship runs through a linter I wrote. It computes contrast ratios from the palette against WCAG AA in both light and dark, and fails the build on a missing alt attribute, an unlabelled form field, a skipped heading level, or a form that collects personal data without linking a privacy notice.

It is open source, so you can read exactly what I am claiming to check. It has caught three real faults in my own work, which is the argument for having it.

Why this matters commercially.

Accessibility is treated as an obligation for businesses in the United States, and demand letters over inaccessible websites are a real and growing cost for small firms. Most of what gets cited is contrast, missing alt text and unlabelled forms, which is exactly what a machine can catch before launch.

This is not legal advice, and no build makes anyone immune. It does mean the cheap, common faults are gone before your site is public.

Built once, then looked after

A site nobody maintains is a site that breaks quietly. After launch I hold the hosting, the domain, the forms and whatever else your site needs on one monthly plan, and that plan includes a set allowance of changes so you can actually get things updated instead of saving them up for a year.
